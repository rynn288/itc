The journey

Filipino cooking is built on a simple argument between sour, salty, and sweet. Vinegar cuts through fat, soy sauce and fish sauce carry the salt, and a spoon of sugar or a squeeze of calamansi settles the difference. Once you can taste that argument, every region starts to make sense.

This trip ran from Manila down to Iloilo and finished in Cebu. Each stop below is a real meal, in the order it happened, with what it cost and what I would order again.

Where the food came from

  • Manila — night market skewers and a late bowl of arroz caldo.
  • Pampanga — sisig on a sizzling plate, still the loudest dish in the country.
  • Iloilo — La Paz batchoy, eaten standing up at 7 in the morning.
  • Cebu — lechon carved at the roadside, no sauce needed.

Dishes to try

Three dishes carried the whole trip. Each one is described below, from the mildest to the most demanding.

Adobo

Meat simmered in vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, peppercorns, and bay leaf until the sauce thickens around it. Every household claims a different version, and all of them are correct. Start here if this is your first Filipino meal.

Sinigang

A sour soup soured with tamarind, guava, or green mango, depending on the cook. It arrives steaming and tastes like it is doing you good. Order it on a rainy afternoon.

Sisig

Chopped pork, onion, and chili on a plate hot enough to keep cooking at the table, finished with calamansi. Loud, rich, and best shared with other people and cold drinks.

Cook it at home

Chicken adobo needs one pan and about forty minutes. The ingredients can go into the pan in any order, so they are an unordered list. The steps cannot, so they are numbered.

Chicken adobo

Serves 4 40 minutes One pan

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken thighs, bone-in
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce
  • 1/2 cup cane vinegar
  • 1 whole head of garlic, crushed
  • 1 teaspoon whole black peppercorns
  • 3 dried bay leaves
  • 1 teaspoon brown sugar
  • 1 cup water

Steps

  1. Marinate the chicken in soy sauce, garlic, and peppercorns for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Sear the chicken in a hot pan, skin side down, until golden. Set it aside.
  3. Pour in the marinade, vinegar, bay leaves, and water. Let it boil without stirring for 2 minutes so the vinegar loses its raw edge.
  4. Return the chicken, cover, and simmer on low heat for 25 minutes.
  5. Uncover, stir in the sugar, and reduce the sauce until it coats the back of a spoon.
  6. Rest for 5 minutes, then serve with hot rice.

Before you go

Four things I wish someone had told me on the first day.

  • Rice is the meal. Everything else is a companion to it.
  • Carinderias with a queue of construction workers at noon are the safe bet.
  • Ask for calamansi and chili on the side, then adjust the dish yourself.
  • Say yes to the dessert you cannot identify.
